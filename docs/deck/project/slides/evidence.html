THE RESEARCH

Output went up. Delivery mostly didn't.

+98% PRs · +91% review

Faros AI, 10,000+ developers: high-AI teams merged twice the PRs and waited far longer for review. No org-level DORA gain.

1.7× issues per PR

CodeRabbit, 470 open-source PRs: AI-authored changes carried more issues, logic and correctness errors most of all.

19% slower, felt faster

METR trial, early-2025 tools: experienced developers were slower with AI but believed they'd been 20% faster.

AI is an amplifier

DORA 2025, ~5,000 professionals: AI magnifies a team's existing strengths and its existing weaknesses.

Faros AI (2025) · CodeRabbit (Dec 2025) · METR (Jul 2025) · DORA State of AI-assisted Software Development (2025)

Four sources, one pattern. Faros AI's telemetry across 10,000+ developers and 1,255 teams: teams with high AI adoption completed 21% more tasks and merged 98% more PRs, but PR review time rose 91%, PRs got 154% bigger, and org-level DORA metrics didn't measurably improve. That's the game's agent trap in real data: more doing, same constraint. CodeRabbit: about 1.7× as many issues per AI-authored PR (10.8 against 6.5), with logic and correctness issues about 75% more common. CodeRabbit sells AI review, so weigh that, but the direction matches everything else. METR ran a randomized trial with 16 experienced open-source developers on their own repos, February to June 2025. With AI they took 19% longer, and afterwards believed they had been 20% faster. Be fair: METR said in February 2026 they believe the speedup is likely better now, and they redesigned the study because developers increasingly refuse to work without AI. The lasting lesson is the perception gap, not the exact number. DORA 2025: AI doesn't fix a team, it amplifies what's already there. Strong platforms and clear workflows get better; weak ones get worse faster.
